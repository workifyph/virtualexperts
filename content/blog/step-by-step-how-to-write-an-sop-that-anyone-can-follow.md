Most business owners know they need Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). But most SOPs are completely useless. They are usually 20-page walls of text hidden away in a forgotten Google Drive folder, written in confusing, academic language that nobody actually reads.

When your team ignores your SOPs, they end up knocking on your digital door, asking the same questions over and over again.

A great SOP isn't a textbook; it is a tactical, idiot-proof recipe. If written correctly, a stranger should be able to step into your business, open the document, and execute the task perfectly on the first try.

Here is the simple, step-by-step framework to write SOPs that anyone can follow.

### Step 1: Record First, Write Later

The biggest mistake people make is trying to write an SOP from scratch in their head. You will inevitably forget minor details—like where a hidden button is located or a critical step in a software login process—because you do it automatically.

- **The Action:** The next time you perform the task, turn on a screen recorder like **Loom**, **Vidyard**, or **Tango**. Talk out loud. Explain *what* you are clicking, *why* you are choosing that option, and what to do if a common error pops up.

This video will serve as the raw data for your written SOP.

### Step 2: Structure with the 4 Essential Elements

Every effective SOP must follow a predictable layout. Skip the corporate fluff and organize your document using these four distinct sections:

1. **The metadata:** Title, author, date created, and the software/tools required.
1. **The "Why" (The Objective):** One sentence explaining why this task matters and what the successful outcome looks like.
1. **The "How" (The Steps):** The chronological, numbered steps to complete the task.
1. **The Quality Control (The Definition of Done):** A final checklist to ensure the work matches your standards before they close the task.

### Step 3: Write Using the "Action-First" Method

When you get to the actual steps, keep your language aggressively simple. Use short sentences and start every single numbered step with a direct action verb.

> **Bad Example:** *The marketing spreadsheet needs to be opened, and then the weekly data from the dashboard should be copied over by the user into column B.*
> **Good Example:** > 1. **Open** the "Weekly Marketing Metrics" Google Sheet. 2. **Navigate** to your Stripe dashboard and copy the "Gross Revenue" figure. 3. **Paste** that figure into cell B2 of the spreadsheet.

### Step 4: Embed Visual Scaffolding

Text alone is a recipe for misinterpretation. People are visual learners. You need to break up the text with clear, contextual visuals.

- **Take screenshots** of exactly what the user should see.
- **Use markup tools** to draw bright red arrows or boxes around the specific buttons they need to click.
- **Embed the raw Loom video** you recorded in Step 1 right at the top of the document so they can watch the workflow in real-time if they get stuck.

### Step 5: The "Stress-Test" (The Ultimate Truth)

Before you declare an SOP complete, you have to test it. Do not give it to the person who already knows how to do the job.

Hand the document to a team member who has **never** performed that specific task before. Sit back, don't say a word, and watch them try to follow it.

Wherever they hesitate, stumble, or ask a question is a spot where your SOP is unclear. Fix those gaps immediately, and you will officially have a bulletproof system.

## Scaling Through Systems

Writing SOPs can feel tedious in the moment, but it is the ultimate form of business leverage. Every hour you spend documenting a process today is dozens of hours you win back in the future. Stop being the bottleneck in your own business—start building your playbook.
