Hiring your first Virtual Assistant (VA) is a massive milestone. It means your business is growing, and you are finally ready to step out of the daily grind and into the role of a true CEO.

But a common mistake many business owners make is thinking the work stops once the contract is signed. If you bring a VA into a chaotic environment without any preparation, they won't save you time—they will just absorb your confusion.

To ensure your investment pays off from day one, you need to prepare. Use this comprehensive checklist to get everything ready before your new VA officially hits the clock.

### Phase 1: Access & Security Prep

The biggest bottleneck on day one is always passwords and permissions. Don't waste your assistant's first week locked out of the systems they need to manage.

- [ ] **Set up a password manager:** Never send raw passwords via email or Slack. Set up a secure sharing tool like **1Password** or **LastPass** so you can share access without revealing your actual master passwords.
- [ ] **Create a dedicated company email:** Set up an alias or inbox (e.g., `assistant@yourcompany.com` or `vanya@yourcompany.com`) to keep their professional communications centralized and branded.
- [ ] **Map out tool permissions:** Audit the platforms they will need. Add them as a user or manager on software like:
  - Project Management (Asana, ClickUp, Trello)
  - Social Media Schedulers (Buffer, Later, Hootsuite)
  - Cloud Storage (Google Drive, Dropbox)

### Phase 2: Documentation & Training Assets

Your VA cannot read your mind. If you want them to execute tasks exactly how you like them, you need to give them a blueprint.

- [ ] **Define the "Low-Hanging Fruit" tasks:** Pick 2–3 simple, repetitive tasks they can take over immediately to build momentum (e.g., daily inbox filtering, scheduling meetings, uploading blog drafts).
- [ ] **Record standard operating procedures (SOPs):** You don't need a 50-page manual. Use a screen-recording tool like **Loom** or **Tango** to record yourself doing the tasks you plan to hand off. Explain your thought process out loud.
- [ ] **Create a Brand & Preference Profile:** Document your stylistic rules so they match your voice.
  - What is your tone (casual, professional, witty)?
  - What are your calendar rules (no meetings on Friday afternoons, always include a 15-minute buffer)?
  - What are your email rules (how to handle refund requests, who gets flagged as a VIP)?

### Phase 3: Communication & Workflow Infrastructure

Without a physical office, you need to deliberately build the digital workspace your VA will inhabit.

- [ ] **Choose your communication hub:** Decide where daily chatter happens. If you use Slack or Microsoft Teams, create an onboarding channel specifically for their questions.
- [ ] **Establish check-in cadences:** Put a recurring meeting on the calendar. A 15-minute stand-up call at the start of the week or a daily text-based update keeps projects moving.
- [ ] **Set up a feedback channel:** Decide how you will give feedback on their early work (e.g., a shared Google Doc for edits or quick Loom video reviews).

## Slow Down to Speed Up

Taking a few hours to run through this checklist before your VA starts might feel like it’s delaying your relief. But in reality, proactive preparation is the only way to ensure your onboarding is smooth, stress-free, and profitable.

By building the foundation today, you ensure your new virtual assistant can confidently hit the ground running tomorrow.
