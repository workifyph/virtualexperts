As a business owner, your company is your baby. You’ve built it from the ground up, poured your sweat and tears into it, and know every moving part.

But as your business grows, holding onto every single task becomes a recipe for burnout. You know you need to delegate—yet, so many entrepreneurs find themselves frustrated when the tasks they pass off don't get done correctly.

If you've ever thought, *"It’s just faster if I do it myself,"* you might be falling into common delegation traps. Here are three things business owners frequently get wrong when delegating tasks—and how to fix them.

## 1. Handing Off the "What" Without the "Why" or "How"

Many owners mistake delegation for simply dumping a task onto someone else's plate. They provide a vague directive like, *"Can you take care of our monthly newsletter?"* and walk away.

Without context, your team member is left guessing. They don't know the ultimate goal (the **why**), nor do they have a standard operating procedure (the **how**).

> **The Fix:** Create clear Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for recurring tasks. When assigning something new, explain how success will be measured and how it fits into the company’s bigger picture.

## 2. Mistaking Delegation for Abdication

There is a massive difference between *delegating* a task and *abdicating* responsibility for it. When you delegate, you remain accountable for the outcome. Abdication is when you hand off a project and completely disappear until the deadline, assuming everything will turn out perfectly.

This usually leads to one of two extremes:

- **The Crash Landing:** The project is completed entirely wrong, forcing you to redo it at the eleventh hour.
- **Micromanagement:** Out of fear of failure, you hover over your employee's shoulder, defeating the entire purpose of freeing up your time.

> **The Fix:** Establish a feedback loop. Set up milestone check-ins—especially for larger projects—so you can course-correct early without micromanaging the daily process.

## 3. Delegating Tasks Instead of Authority

If you ask an employee to manage a vendor but they have to ask your permission for every $50 spent, you haven’t actually delegated anything; you’ve just added an extra step to your own approval process.

When you don't grant the authority to make decisions, your team will constantly bottleneck your workflow waiting for your "OK." This drains their morale and keeps you trapped in the weeds.

> **The Fix:** Clearly define boundaries of autonomy. Tell your team exactly what decisions they are allowed to make on their own (e.g., *"You have a budget of up to $500 to solve customer complaints without checking with me first"*).

### Shift from Doing to Leading

Delegation isn’t a sign of weakness; it’s the ultimate growth strategy. By avoiding these three pitfalls, you don't just clear your own calendar—you empower your team, build a scalable business, and finally get the freedom to focus on the big picture.
